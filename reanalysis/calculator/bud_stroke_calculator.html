BUD-Stroke Score

Five-year risk of a first stroke for women aged 18–60 with uterine fibroids, adenomyosis or endometriosis (benign uterine disease, BUD) and no previous stroke. Developed in 24,006 women with 345 strokes at one US academic center.

    How well it works, and its limits

    Discrimination. Harrell C 0.696 after optimism correction (200 bootstrap samples); the points score has C 0.697. Temporal validation (developed on diagnoses 2018–2022, tested on 2023–2026): C 0.665 at 3 years.

    Calibration. Predicted and observed risks agree in the development data. In the 2023–2026 validation group the observed 3-year risk was 1.64 times the predicted risk, so the calculator may underestimate risk in newer patients or other settings.

    Predictors. Hypertension, coronary artery disease, migraine, chronic kidney disease, hemoglobinopathy, estrogen-containing hormonal therapy, diabetes and lower hemoglobin carry points. Age, BMI, dyslipidemia, smoking, endometriosis and adenomyosis were in the model but carried no independent points in this cohort (they affect only the exact model risk). Younger age scored slightly higher, which most likely reflects who was tested and coded rather than a real protective effect of age. Smoking was undocumented for two-thirds of women. Race was not used.

    Outcome. Stroke (ischemic, hemorrhagic, cerebral venous thrombosis or unspecified; TIA excluded) identified from diagnosis codes and imaging reports, not chart review. Median follow-up 2.8 years.

    Use. For research and hypothesis generation. It has not been validated in another population and should not guide individual treatment. Anemia found at gynecologic diagnosis warrants evaluation of its cause and of vascular risk factors regardless of the score.
